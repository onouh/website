## Problem

A CPU is easy to draw and hard to trust. Block diagrams hide whether the control unit actually decodes, whether branches actually resolve, and whether hazards actually corrupt state. I wanted a processor I could simulate instruction-by-instruction and watch those questions get answered.

## Approach

I designed a modular 32-bit single-cycle MIPS datapath in VHDL on the Xilinx toolchain: a custom ALU, register file, and distinct instruction/data memory blocks. A hardwired control unit decodes all three MIPS instruction formats — R, I, and J — driving opcode-dependent execution signals and data flow across the processor. Simulation testbenches cover four areas: opcode branching logic, datapath timing constraints, ALU flags, and structural register hazards.

## Outcome

A 32-bit single-cycle processor that simulates end-to-end, with all three instruction formats decoded in hardware and testbench coverage across branching, timing, flags, and hazards. RTL stopped being notation: register widths, control signals, and hazard windows became things I could measure in a waveform, not assume on a slide.

## Stack

VHDL, Vivado / Xilinx, MIPS, ALU, single-cycle datapath, R/I/J decode.
