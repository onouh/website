## Problem

Compiler frontends are usually met through tools: flex/bison generate the code, and the theory stays theory. I wanted the reverse — every automaton hand-built, so tokenization, grammar design, and parsing are things I can debug, not just describe.

## Approach

I built the frontend from scratch in C/C++ across three stages. The lexer is hand-coded from custom regular expressions compiled into Deterministic Finite Automata, tokenizing with explicit error recovery. The grammar is a formal Context-Free Grammar covering four construct classes: functions, control-flow loops, conditionals, and multivariable arithmetic. The parser is recursive-descent — one function per non-terminal — performing strict syntax analysis and generating visual parse trees so structure can be verified at a glance.

## Outcome

A working pipeline from raw characters to parse trees with zero parser-generator dependencies: three stages, four construct classes in the CFG, and error paths designed rather than stumbled into. Building it settled the theory the way theory only settles — by breaking: why DFAs beat backtracking, and why recursive descent mirrors the grammar directly.

## Stack

C, C++, regular expressions, DFAs, CFG, recursive-descent parsing.
